Short Answer Type Questions (15) 15 questions

SHORT • S1FROM YOUR BOOK

1. Enlist the different methods of communication.

Ans: (1) Verbal — oral (speech, phone, meeting) + written (letter, email, report, notice). (2) Non-verbal — expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, tone of voice. (3) Visual — charts, maps, signals, posters, videos.
SHORT • S2FROM YOUR BOOK

2. Which is the easiest method of communication and why?

Ans: Non-verbal is the easiest — it needs no language or literacy. Gestures, expressions and tone are understood naturally (even babies use them) and work across different languages.
SHORT • S3FROM YOUR BOOK

3. Differentiate between verbal and non-verbal communication.

Ans: Verbal uses words (speech, email) — precise and recordable. Non-verbal uses no words (smile, gesture, tone) — instant and emotional. Best communication combines both: words carry facts, body carries feelings.
SHORT • S4FROM YOUR BOOK

4. Explain in your own words how body language affects our communication.

Ans: Body language (expressions, posture, eye contact) carries most of the message's impact and shows true feelings. Eye contact + open posture build trust; crossed arms + no eye contact create distrust — receivers believe the body over words.
SHORT • S5FROM YOUR BOOK

5. What do you understand by visual communication? Give a few examples where it is used.

Ans: Visual communication conveys meaning through pictures/signs instead of words. Examples: traffic signals, road maps, pie charts in reports, no-smoking signs, fest posters, company logos. It is fast and crosses language barriers.
SHORT • S6

6. What is feedback? Why is it called the lifeline of communication?

Ans: Feedback is the receiver's response flowing back to the sender. It is the lifeline because it (1) confirms the message was understood and (2) corrects errors early — completing the two-way loop.
SHORT • S7

7. Name any two barriers to communication with one measure to overcome each.

Ans: (1) Linguistic (French manual) → use simple words + visuals/translator. (2) Physical (classroom noise) → shift to a quiet room with a working mic.
SHORT • S8

8. Explain any two Cs of effective communication with examples.

Ans: (1) Clear — simple, exact message ("Submit the file by Friday 5 pm"). (2) Courteous — polite words ("please", "thank you", "sorry for the delay").
SHORT • S9

9. What is paralanguage? Give two examples.

Ans: Paralanguage is HOW the voice sounds — tone, pitch, speed, pauses (part of non-verbal). Examples: loud-fast voice shows excitement/anger; soft-slow voice shows calmness/sadness. Book fact: only 7% words — 38% voice + 55% body!
SHORT • S10

10. Differentiate encoding and decoding.

Ans: Encoding (sender's job) = converting a thought into words/gestures. Decoding (receiver's job) = interpreting the received message into meaning.
SHORT • S11

11. What is a topic sentence? Why is it important?

Ans: The topic sentence is the first line stating the paragraph's main idea (e.g. "Yoga keeps students healthy."). It guides the reader and keeps the writer's supporting lines on track.
SHORT • S12

12. In "Wow! What a goal!", identify the part of speech of "Wow" and the sentence type.

Ans: "Wow" is an interjection (sudden feeling + "!"). "What a goal!" is an exclamatory sentence.
SHORT • S13

13. State any two advantages of written communication.

Ans: (1) It creates a permanent record/proof that can be re-read and checked. (2) It is formal and reaches far-off people with the exact same message.
SHORT • S14

14. What is constructive feedback? Give one example.

Ans: Constructive feedback points out an error AND suggests a solution — the best type. Example: "Slide 3 is wrong — recheck the figures and use a bigger font."
SHORT • S15

S15. What are articles? Fill in: (a) ___ honest boy (b) ___ Sun rises in the East.

Ans: Articles (a/an/the) point to nouns. (a) An honest boy — "An" before vowel SOUNDS (h is silent). (b) The Sun — "The" for unique/single objects. Rule: A = consonant sound (a book), An = vowel sound (an apple), The = specific/unique (the Taj Mahal).

Long Answer Type Questions (12) 12 questions

LONG • L1FROM YOUR BOOK

16. Discuss in detail the verbal method of communication.

Ans: Verbal = communication using WORDS, in two forms. Oral (speech, phone, meeting, video call): merits — fast, instant feedback, personal touch, doubts cleared at once; demerits — no record, forgotten easily, weak for long data. Written (letter, email, report, notice): merits — permanent proof, re-readable, formal, same message to all; demerits — slow, no instant feedback, needs literacy. Use oral for urgent/personal matters, written for formal/record needs.
LONG • L2FROM YOUR BOOK

17. Explain the various elements of non-verbal communication.

Ans: (1) Facial expressions — smile/frown show feelings instantly. (2) Gestures — wave, thumbs-up, pointing. (3) Posture — straight = confident, slouched = careless. (4) Eye contact — shows honesty and attention. (5) Touch — handshake, pat. (6) Paralanguage — tone, pitch, speed of voice. (7) Appearance/space — dress and distance. Together they carry most impact — receivers trust the body over words.
LONG • L3FROM YOUR BOOK

18. What do you understand by visual communication? Enlist its advantages and disadvantages.

Ans: Visual = meaning through pictures/signs (signals, maps, charts, posters, logos, videos). Advantages: very fast to grasp; understood across languages; attractive and memorable — ideal for ads and teaching. Disadvantages: needs design skill/time; cannot express complex detail alone; may be misread without context; printing/display costs. Uses: traffic control, classrooms (charts), business reports (graphs), advertising (hoardings).
LONG • L4FROM YOUR BOOK

19. Compare verbal and non-verbal communication.

Ans: Medium: words vs body/tone. Record: written-verbal is permanent; non-verbal vanishes. Feedback: oral gives instant reply; non-verbal is read instantly too. Literacy: written needs reading skill; non-verbal needs none. Emotion: non-verbal shows true feelings; words can hide them. Example: "I'm fine" in an angry tone — words say fine, body says upset; wise listeners trust the body.
LONG • L5

20. Explain the communication cycle with an example.

Ans: The 7-step loop: Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback (feedback arrow returns to sender). Example: teacher explains photosynthesis (sender/encoding) → speaks in class (message/channel) → student understands (decoding) → student nods/asks a doubt (feedback). If feedback shows confusion, the sender re-explains — the loop continues till understanding is confirmed.
LONG • L6

21. Discuss the significance of feedback and the qualities of good feedback.

Ans: Significance (6): confirms understanding; corrects errors early; completes one-way talk into two-way communication; improves performance; builds trust and relations; helps the sender improve. Good feedback is: specific (exact point), timely (soon), descriptive (facts, no insults), constructive (suggest a fix), polite, and about the WORK not the person ("the report needs dates", not "you are careless"). Supportive feedback also encourages and offers continuing help.
LONG • L7

22. Explain any four barriers to communication with examples and measures to overcome them.

Ans: (1) Physical — class noise → quiet room, working mic. (2) Linguistic — French manual → simple words + visuals. (3) Cultural — thumbs-up rude abroad → learn customs. (4) Emotional — anger blocks listening → stay calm, right time. Plus two general rules: always listen actively with feedback, and summarise key points at the end. Book note: the CBSE book groups emotional + perceptual factors under Interpersonal barriers (tone, emotions, personal biases).
LONG • L8

23. Explain the 7 Cs of effective communication with one example each.

Ans: Clear — "Submit file by Friday 5 pm". Concise — "Meeting at 10". Concrete — "Sales rose 20%". Correct — checked dates/grammar. Coherent — First→Next→Finally order. Complete — invite with date+time+venue+contact. Courteous — "please/thank you". Trick: "Can Cows Chew Cold Corn Cob Candy?"
LONG • L9

L9. Describe basic writing skills: sentences, phrases and paragraphs.

Ans: Sentence = Subject + Predicate, CAPITAL start, ./ ? / ! end; 4 kinds — declarative, interrogative, imperative, exclamatory. Phrase = group of words with NO complete meaning (no subject+verb), e.g. "in the morning" — phrases BUILD sentences. Paragraph = topic sentence → 3–4 supporting lines → concluding line; one idea each. Add articles (a/an/the), capitals, punctuation and logical order.
LONG • L10

25. What is active listening? Give six do's of a good listener.

Ans: Active listening = fully concentrating to understand the speaker and respond thoughtfully (not just hearing). Six do's: (1) face the speaker with eye contact; (2) never interrupt; (3) nod to show attention; (4) paraphrase — "So you mean…?"; (5) ask clarifying questions; (6) note key points. It prevents perceptual barriers and builds trust.
LONG • L11

26. How do you choose the right method of communication? Explain with four situations.

Ans: Decide by urgency, distance, audience, feelings involved and whether a record is needed. (1) Urgent doubt nearby → oral (instant). (2) Leave application → written (proof). (3) Upset friend → face-to-face (tone + care). (4) Stranger, unknown language → visual (map/signs). Combining methods (report + chart + talk) works best for important messages.
LONG • L12

L12. Explain the 9 parts of speech (5 basic + 4 supporting) with one example each.

Ans: Basic 5: Noun (Amit, book) • Pronoun (he, they) • Verb (run, write) • Adjective (tall boy) • Adverb (runs fast). Supporting 4: Article (a, an, the — points to nouns) • Preposition (book on table) • Conjunction (and, because) • Interjection (Wow!). Article picks which noun: a boy (any) vs the boy (that one). Sample: "Wow! (interjection) Amit (noun) runs (verb) very (adverb) fast (adverb) and (conjunction) wins." Correct usage = correct (C) writing.

Application Based Questions (12) 12 questions

APPLICATION • A1FROM YOUR BOOK

28. When you see an e-mail full of grammatical errors or incomplete sentences, how do you react? Does it affect your perception of the sender? Why or why not?

Ans: I struggle to understand it and my respect for the sender drops — errors signal carelessness and break the Correct and Clear principles. But I stay fair: if the sender is a non-native speaker, I focus on the content and politely ask for clarity instead of judging.
APPLICATION • A2FROM YOUR BOOK

29. How aware are you of your own body language? Has your body language ever got you in trouble while communicating with someone?

Ans: I now watch my habits — eye contact, no fidgeting, open posture. Once in a viva I sat cross-armed (I was only nervous) and the teacher thought me rude — my marks suffered. Lesson learnt: the body speaks first, so I keep a smile, straight posture and calm hands.
APPLICATION • A3FROM YOUR BOOK

30. You are the CEO of a company. Which type of communication would you choose to convey the rules and regulations to the employees and why?

Ans: Written (official circular + email + notice board) — rules need identical wording for all, permanent proof and re-reading. I would follow it with a short oral town-hall to explain and clear doubts. Written = authority + record; oral = understanding.
APPLICATION • A4FROM YOUR BOOK

31. Imagine you have to justify that Virat Kohli is a better captain than MS Dhoni to a group of audience. Which method of communication will be most appropriate for this justification and why?

Ans: Oral public communication (live speech/debate) supported by visuals (win-% charts, chase records on screen). Persuasion needs voice modulation, gestures, eye contact and instant Q&A — plus data visuals for proof. Written text alone cannot win over a live crowd.
APPLICATION • A5

32. Riya messages her team "do it soon". Identify the violated C and rewrite the message correctly.

Ans: Violates Clear (vague, no deadline) and Complete (no what/when). Rewrite: "Please submit the sales sheet by Friday 5 pm on email." — now clear, complete and courteous.
APPLICATION • A6

33. A team leader's juniors look confused by his technical jargon. What two steps should he take?

Ans: (1) Replace jargon with simple words + show a chart/demo (attacks the linguistic barrier). (2) Pause, ask "any doubts?", and paraphrase replies to confirm (feedback + active listening).
APPLICATION • A7

34. For the school fest, Class X puts up posters, announces on loudspeaker, and sends a written notice to parents. Name the 3 methods and explain why combining beats using one.

Ans: Posters = visual; loudspeaker = oral; notice = written. Combination wins: visual attracts, oral spreads fast, written is proof — together covering busy, far-off and record-needing audiences.
APPLICATION • A8

35. Your best friend is upset after failing a test. Which method of communication will you choose and why?

Ans: Face-to-face oral + non-verbal — sit together, listen patiently, keep a caring tone and eye contact. Feelings need warmth and instant response; a cold text message cannot console anyone.
APPLICATION • A9

36. You must present monthly sales to your manager. What combination will you use and why?

Ans: Written report + visual charts, with a short oral summary in the meeting. Report = permanent record for the file; charts = one-glance understanding; oral talk = doubts cleared instantly.
APPLICATION • A10

37. A stranger speaking an unknown language asks you for directions. What will you do?

Ans: Switch to visual + gestures: draw a simple map, point left/right, show fingers for distance, or use a translator/map app. Speak slowly with a smile — patience plus pictures beats the language barrier.
APPLICATION • A11

38. Your friend says "I'm fine" in an angry tone with crossed arms. What do you believe and what will you do?

Ans: I believe the body, not the words — tone + crossed arms contradict "fine" (non-verbal carries true feelings). I will gently say, "You seem upset — want to talk?" and listen without forcing.
APPLICATION • A12

39. A school trip notice misses the date and venue. Which C is violated? Rewrite the notice correctly.

Ans: Violates Complete (missing when/where) and Clear. Rewrite: "Trip to Lotus Temple on Tue 16 Dec. Bus leaves 8 am, returns 4 pm. Submit consent + Rs. 200 by Friday. — Class Teacher."

Competency Based Questions (12) 12 questions

COMPETENCY • C1FROM YOUR BOOK*
Ravi normally shares his thoughts with Ram face-to-face, and they discuss and reply to each other. Which form of communication is this — (i) Interpersonal Communication or (ii) Written Communication? Why? (Photo was cut; answered as read.)
Ans: (i) Interpersonal Communication — it happens between two people, face-to-face, with instant two-way exchange (talk + reply + body language). Written would be one-way and delayed, with no discussion.
COMPETENCY • C2
Amit presents with zero eye contact, 40 text-packed slides in 10 minutes, and says "no questions, I am getting late". The manager understands nothing. (a) Identify 4 communication mistakes. (b) Suggest 4 fixes.
Ans: (a) No eye contact (poor non-verbal) • overloaded slides (not concise) • too fast (not clear) • no questions (blocks feedback). (b) Eye contact + open posture • max 10 slides, 1 idea each • slow clear speech • invite questions + summarise at end.
COMPETENCY • C3
In a job interview, a candidate gives no eye contact, sits slouched, and mumbles answers. (a) Identify 3 non-verbal problems and the impression each creates. (b) Give 3 fixes.
Ans: (a) No eye contact → seems dishonest/nervous • slouching → seems careless • mumbling → seems unprepared. (b) Steady eye contact + smile • straight, open posture • clear, slow speech with prepared short answers.
COMPETENCY • C4
Two teams advertise a blood-donation camp: Team A only chats about it in class; Team B puts up posters, announces in assembly, and sends a parent circular. (a) Which team communicates better? (b) Name Team B's 3 methods and one strength of each.
Ans: (a) Team B — wider reach + record. (b) Posters = visual (attracts, memorable) • assembly = oral (fast, whole school at once) • circular = written (proof for parents). Team A's oral-only chat reaches few and is forgotten.
COMPETENCY • C5
A teacher compares two "My Aim" answers — A: one 20-line unbroken block; B: 3 short paragraphs, each starting with a clear point. (a) Whose answer is better? (b) List 4 paragraph rules B followed.
Ans: (a) B — broken into readable idea-chunks. (b) One idea per paragraph • topic sentence first • 3–4 supporting lines • concluding line (+ capitals + full stops). A's wall of text hides ideas; B's structure shows them.
COMPETENCY • C6
In a project team, members keep interrupting, assume meanings without asking, and never review each other's work. (a) Name 3 barriers/skills violated. (b) Give a 3-point fix.
Ans: (a) Interpersonal (interruptions) • Perceptual (assumptions) • No feedback (no review). (b) Turn-taking rule + listen fully • paraphrase before replying ("So you mean…?") • weekly written minutes + peer review of drafts.
COMPETENCY • C7
A teacher speaks very fast, uses no examples or visuals, and allows no doubts in class. Students fail the test. (a) Identify 3 teaching-communication mistakes. (b) Suggest 3 fixes.
Ans: (a) Too fast (not clear) • no visuals/examples (tough ideas stay abstract) • no questions (feedback loop cut). (b) Slow pace + daily-life examples • charts/demo/models • doubt breaks every 10 minutes + end-of-class recap.
COMPETENCY • C8
Advertise (a) a village fair and (b) an online gadget. For EACH, choose the best method-combo and justify in one line.
Ans: (a) Village fair: loudspeaker announcements (oral) + painted banners (visual) — reaches low-literacy crowds fast and cheap. (b) Online gadget: demo video + infographics (visual) + written reviews — buyers compare specs visually before paying. Rule: audience decides the method.
COMPETENCY • C9
You must convey sad news (a pet's death) to your little cousin. Options: text message, phone call, or going in person. Choose one and justify with 3 reasons.
Ans: Go in person (face-to-face). (1) Sensitive news needs caring tone + expressions (non-verbal). (2) You can hug, console and answer questions instantly. (3) Text/phone hide feelings and feel cold — the proper channel for emotional news is always personal.
COMPETENCY • C10
In a group discussion, one student dominates, one stays silent, and two argue without listening. Suggest 3 rules to make this GD effective.
Ans: (1) Listen, then speak — no interrupting; build on others' points. (2) Invite all — "Sara, what's your view?" + 1-minute turns so none dominates. (3) Respect + summarise — disagree politely with reasons; one member recaps decisions at the end.
COMPETENCY • C11
The hostel warden must announce an urgent midnight water-cut for tomorrow. Options: notice board, WhatsApp broadcast, morning assembly announcement. Choose the best combo and justify.
Ans: WhatsApp broadcast + notice board + warden announcement combined. Broadcast = instant reach tonight (speed); board = written proof nobody can deny; announcement = authority + doubts cleared. Urgent + important news always deserves more than one channel.
COMPETENCY • C12
During exams, the invigilator only uses gestures — finger on lips for silence, pointing to seats, showing the clock for time. (a) Identify the method. (b) Give 2 merits of it HERE and 1 limitation.
Ans: (a) Non-verbal communication. (b) Merits: fully silent (zero disturbance) + instantly understood by all (universal gestures). Limitation: cannot explain complex rules (e.g. new marking scheme) — those still need clear written/oral instructions.

SHORT • S18

SHORT • S22

Ans: One speaker addressing a LARGE audience in planned form. Example: principal's speech in morning assembly.
SHORT • S23

Ans: (1) Fast with instant feedback and doubt-clearing. (2) Personal touch — tone + expressions add meaning.
SHORT • S24

SHORT • S32

SHORT • S33

SHORT • S38

Ans: Sentences giving orders/requests. Examples: "Open page 10." / "Please sit down."
SHORT • S40

SHORT • S41

S41. Identify the parts of speech: "quickly" and "beautiful". Use each in a phrase.

SHORT • S42

Ans: Conjunctions join words/sentences (and, but, because). Joined: "It rained, but we played."
SHORT • S43

Ans: Prepositions show relation/position (in, on, at, under). Answer: "The book is on the table."
SHORT • S44

Ans: A phrase = group of words with NO complete meaning (no subject+verb): "in the morning", "on the table". A sentence = complete meaning with subject + predicate ("Birds fly in the morning."). Phrases are sentence building-blocks.
SHORT • S45

S45. State two capitalization rules with examples.

Ans: (1) Start every sentence with a capital: "We won." (2) Capitalise names/places/days/"I": "Amit, Delhi, Monday, I".

Long Answer — Set 2 (L13–L32) 20 questions

LONG • L13

L13. Explain oral communication: meaning, examples, merits, demerits and best uses.

Ans: Oral = spoken words (talk, phone, speech, meeting, video call). Merits: fast, instant feedback, personal, doubts cleared live, body language adds meaning. Demerits: no record, forgotten/distorted, poor for numbers and legal proof. Best uses: urgent matters, discussions, counselling, persuasion, quick coordination.
LONG • L14

L14. Explain written communication: meaning, examples, merits, demerits and best uses.

Ans: Written = reading/writing (letter, email, report, notice, books, SMS). Merits: permanent proof, re-readable, identical message to all, formal, works over distance/time. Demerits: slow, no instant feedback, needs literacy, tone can be misread. Best uses: rules, orders, applications, records, far-off formal messages.
LONG • L15

L15. Describe intrapersonal, interpersonal, small-group and public communication with one example each.

Ans: Intrapersonal — with self: rehearsing answers mentally. Interpersonal — between two: friend consoling friend. Small-group — 3–12 interacting: project team of 5 planning. Public — one speaker to masses: principal's assembly speech. Size grows: self → two → few → many.
LONG • L16

L16. Explain the communication cycle step-by-step with a classroom example.

Ans: (1) Sender (teacher) has an idea. (2) Encoding: converts it into simple words + diagram. (3) Message: the explanation itself. (4) Channel: speech + board. (5) Decoding: students interpret. (6) Receiver: the class. (7) Feedback: nods/doubts flow back; confused looks make the teacher re-explain — loop till all understand.
LONG • L17

L17. What are the types of feedback? Explain any four with examples.

Ans: Positive: "Great chart!" (praise). Negative: "Two totals are wrong." (error only). Constructive: "Totals wrong — re-add column B." (error + fix — best). Descriptive: "You spoke 5 minutes." (facts only). Others: specific vs vague, immediate (nod) vs delayed (result), formal (report card) vs informal (smile). Also No feedback (silence — itself signals disagreement).
LONG • L18

L18. Why is feedback called a two-way process? Explain with an example.

Ans: Message flows sender→receiver (way 1); feedback flows receiver→sender (way 2) — both sides send AND receive. Example: manager assigns a task (way 1) → employee asks "deadline Friday?" (way 2) → manager confirms (way 1 again). This to-and-fro continues till both agree — hence two-way.
LONG • L19

L19. Explain physical, linguistic and cultural barriers with one example + one fix each.

Ans: Physical: generator noise in class → quiet room + mic. Linguistic: English notice for Hindi-only parents → bilingual notice + pictures. Cultural: left-hand giving offends some guests → learn and respect local customs. Rule: match environment, language and manners to the audience.
LONG • L20

L20. Explain emotional, perceptual, organisational and interpersonal barriers with one example each.

Ans: Emotional: sobbing child hears no scolding. Perceptual: "contract staff are lazy" — judging before listening. Organisational: peon cannot reach director through 4 levels. Interpersonal: quarrelling partners stop talking. Fixes: right time, open mind, open-door policy, polite rebuilding of trust. Book note: the CBSE book files emotional + perceptual factors under Interpersonal barriers.
LONG • L21

L21. Describe 8 measures to overcome communication barriers.

Ans: ① Simple clear language for the audience. ② Active listening (eye contact, no interruption, "So you mean…?"). ③ Right medium (urgent→call, proof→email). ④ Remove disturbances (silence phone, quiet room). ⑤ Give + demand feedback. ⑥ Visuals for tough ideas. ⑦ Calm polite confident behaviour. ⑧ Repeat/summarise key points at the end.
LONG • L22

L22. Explain Clear, Concise and Concrete with examples — and what goes wrong if each is violated.

Ans: Clear: "Submit by Friday 5 pm" — vague "soon" causes missed deadlines. Concise: "Meeting at 10" — 3-page mail for it wastes time and hides the point. Concrete: "Rose 20%" — "rose a lot" convinces nobody and invites doubt. Together they make messages exact, quick and believable.
LONG • L23

L23. Explain Correct, Coherent, Complete and Courteous with one example each.

Ans: Correct: verified date/grammar ("Exam on 16 Dec", checked). Coherent: First pay fees → Next submit form → Finally revise. Complete: invite with date+time+venue+contact. Courteous: "Please send the file; thank you!" Correctness builds trust, order builds sense, completeness avoids follow-ups, courtesy earns cooperation.
LONG • L24

L24. Explain attention, consistency, timeliness and proper channel with one example each.

Ans: Attention: keep phone aside while the teacher explains. Consistency: practise the punctuality you preach. Timeliness: announce the trip a week early, not last night. Proper channel: console face-to-face, never by SMS. These four decide WHETHER your (even correct) message actually works.
LONG • L25

L25. Describe the 4 sentence types with two examples each.

Ans: Declarative (statement + .): "I study IT." / "Delhi is big." Interrogative (question + ?): "Where is the file?" / "Did you eat?" Imperative (order/request): "Open page 10." / "Please sit." Exclamatory (feeling + !): "What a goal!" / "How fast she runs!" Every sentence = Subject + Predicate with CAPITAL start.
LONG • L26

L26. Explain nouns, pronouns and verbs with examples, then write one sentence using all three.

Ans: Noun = name (Amit, Delhi, book). Pronoun = noun-replacer (he, she, they, it). Verb = action/state (run, is, write). Sentence: "Amit (noun) writes (verb) fast, and he (pronoun) always finishes first."
LONG • L27

L27. Explain adjectives, adverbs and prepositions with examples, then write one sentence using all three.

Ans: Adjective describes nouns (tall boy, red pen). Adverb describes verbs/adjectives (runs fast, very tall). Preposition shows relation (in, on, at, under). Sentence: "The tall (adj) boy runs quickly (adv) to (prep) school."
LONG • L28

L28. How do you write a good paragraph? Explain the 3 parts and write a 4-line paragraph on "Yoga".

Ans: Parts: ① Topic sentence (main idea) ② 3–4 supporting details/examples ③ Concluding line; one idea per paragraph. Sample: "Yoga keeps students healthy. It stretches the body, calms the mind and sharpens focus. Just 20 minutes daily improves sleep and memory. So every student should practise yoga every morning."
LONG • L29

L29. What are the two parts of a sentence? Identify subject and predicate in: (a) The little girl laughed loudly. (b) My father reads the newspaper. (c) Open your books.

Ans: Subject (who/what) + Predicate (what about them = verb + rest; the book also names verb + object inside it). (a) S: The little girl | P: laughed loudly (verb: laughed). (b) S: My father | P: reads the newspaper (verb: reads, object: newspaper). (c) S: (You — hidden) | P: Open your books.
LONG • L30

L30. Explain 6 punctuation marks with one use each.

Ans: Full stop (.) ends statements ("I came."). Comma (,) separates items/pauses ("apples, bananas"). Question (?) ends questions. Exclamation (!) shows feeling ("Wow!"). Apostrophe (') shows missing letters ("don't") and possession ("Ram's"). Colon (:) starts lists ("Bring: pen, file").
LONG • L31

L31. Compare oral and written communication on 4 points.

Ans: Speed/feedback: oral instant; written delayed. Record: oral vanishes; written is permanent proof. Suitability: oral for urgent/personal; written for formal/complex/far. Skill needed: oral needs speaking+listening; written needs reading+writing. Smart communicators mix both.
LONG • L32

L32. A shy student wants to participate more in class. Give 6 communication tips.

Ans: ① Prepare one question/answer daily (intrapersonal rehearsal). ② Sit in front; keep eye contact + nod (non-verbal confidence). ③ Start small — answer easy questions first. ④ Speak slowly and clearly (paralanguage). ⑤ Ask doubts without fear — teachers love feedback. ⑥ Join one group activity weekly (small-group practice).
