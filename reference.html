Topic 1: Methods of Communication

Meaning: Methods (modes) are the ways we exchange information, ideas and feelings. There are 3 main methods.

(a) Verbal communication — using WORDS (2 types)

TypeMeansExamplesAdvantagesDisadvantages
Oral / SpokenTalking + listeningFace-to-face talk, phone/video call, speech, meeting, classroom teachingFast, instant feedback, personal touch, doubts cleared at onceNo permanent record, forgotten easily, not good for long/complex data
WrittenReading + writingLetter, email, SMS, report, notice, books, WhatsApp messagePermanent proof/record, can re-read, formal, reaches farSlow, no instant feedback, needs literacy, tone can be misunderstood

(b) Non-verbal communication — WITHOUT words (body language)

Facial expressions (smile, frown), gestures (wave, thumbs-up), posture (straight = confident), eye contact, touch (handshake), tone/pitch/speed of voice (called paralanguage), appearance/dress. Experts say body + tone carry most of the message's impact — words alone are not enough. Example: saying "I'm fine" in an angry tone + crossed arms = receiver believes the body, not the words.

(c) Visual communication — using PICTURES/SIGNS

Charts, graphs, maps, traffic signals, 🚫 no-smoking sign, logos, photos, videos, emojis. Biggest plus: crosses language barriers — a red cross or arrow is understood everywhere. Example: a pie chart shows "60% passed" faster than a paragraph.

Choosing the right method

SituationBest methodWhy
Urgent doubt with teacher nearbyOralInstant reply
Leave application to principalWritten (email/letter)Formal record needed
Teaching upset friendFace-to-face (oral + non-verbal)Feelings need tone + expressions
Road directions for strangerVisual (map/sign)Language may differ
Monthly sales to managerWritten + Visual (report + chart)Record + quick understanding
Exam line: "There are 3 methods — verbal (oral + written), non-verbal and visual. The choice depends on urgency, distance, audience and whether a record is needed."

Topic 2: The Significance of Feedback in Communication Cycle

The communication cycle (7 steps — draw in exam)

Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel/Medium → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback

  • Encoding = sender converts thought into words/gestures. Decoding = receiver interprets it.
  • Example: Teacher explains photosynthesis (sender/encoding) → speaks in class (message/channel) → student understands (decoding) → student nods / asks doubt (feedback).

What is feedback?

The receiver's response that flows BACK to the sender. It completes the loop and proves the message was received + understood. Without feedback, communication is one-way (like shouting into a tunnel).

Types of feedback

TypeMeaningExample
PositivePraise/appreciation"Great presentation, well done!"
Negative / CriticalPoints out error"Slide 3 data is wrong."
ConstructiveError + solution (BEST type)"Slide 3 is wrong — recheck sales figures and use bigger font."
DescriptiveStates facts, no judging"You spoke for 12 minutes with 10 slides."
SpecificPinpoints exact point"Your introduction was clear." (vs vague "good job")
Immediate / DelayedInstant vs laterNod in class vs exam result after a month

Significance — why feedback matters (learn 6)

  1. Confirms understanding — sender knows message reached correctly.
  2. Corrects errors early — wrong data/impression fixed before damage.
  3. Completes the cycle — turns one-way talk into two-way communication.
  4. Improves performance — students/employees learn and grow.
  5. Builds trust & relations — honest feedback shows care and respect.
  6. Helps the sender improve — teacher adjusts speed, speaker improves style.

Qualities of GOOD feedback

Specific (exact point) • Timely (soon) • Descriptive (facts, not insults) • Constructive (suggest fix) • Polite/courteous • About the WORK, not the person ("the report needs dates" ❌ not "you are careless").

Topic 3: Measures to Overcome Barriers in Communication

The 7 barriers (with example each)

BarrierMeaningExample
1. PhysicalEnvironment disturbancesNoise in class, bad phone network, long distance
2. Linguistic / LanguageDifferent languages, jargon, tough wordsFrench manual for Hindi-speaking worker; doctor's Latin terms
3. CulturalDifferent customs/gestures/values👍 means "good" in India but is rude in some countries
4. EmotionalFeelings block listeningAngry student can't hear teacher's advice; stage fear
5. PerceptualDifferent viewpoints/assumptions"He is always late" — judging before listening
6. OrganisationalCompany structure blocks flowStrict hierarchy — worker can't reach manager; no suggestion box
7. InterpersonalPoor relations between peopleDistrust, interruptions, jealousy between teammates

Measures to overcome them (barrier → solution)

BarrierMeasures
PhysicalSilent room, working mic/network, right distance, proper seating/lighting
LinguisticSimple words, short sentences, common language, explain jargon, use translator/visuals
CulturalLearn other's customs/greetings, respect festivals, avoid offensive gestures
EmotionalStay calm, control anger, listen patiently, build confidence, right time to talk
PerceptualListen fully before judging, keep open mind, ask questions, paraphrase to confirm
OrganisationalOpen-door policy, suggestion boxes, clear channels, regular meetings, feedback system
InterpersonalBe polite, don't interrupt, respect others, build trust, active listening

8 golden general measures (for any 2/4-marker)

  1. Use simple, clear language — know your audience.
  2. Listen actively — eye contact, no interruption, paraphrase ("So you mean…?").
  3. Choose the right medium (urgent → call; record → email).
  4. Remove physical disturbances (silence phone, quiet room).
  5. Always give and ask for feedback.
  6. Use visuals (charts, demos) for tough ideas.
  7. Control emotions; be polite, patient and confident.
  8. Repeat/summarise key points at the end.

Topic 4: The Principles of Communication (The 7 Cs + more)

Good communication follows fixed principles. The most famous = 7 Cs:

#PrincipleMeaningExample
1ClearSimple words, one idea at a time✅ "Submit the file by Friday 5 pm" ❌ "Do it soon-ish"
2ConciseShort, no extra words✅ "Meeting at 10" ❌ "I wanted to tell you that there will be a meeting…"
3ConcreteSpecific facts/figures✅ "Sales rose 20%" ❌ "Sales rose a lot"
4CorrectRight grammar, facts, spellingCheck dates, names, numbers before sending
5CoherentLogical order, linked sentencesFirst → Next → Finally (not jumping randomly)
6CompleteAll needed info: what, when, where, whomInvite with date + time + venue + contact
7CourteousPolite, respectful"Please", "thank you", "sorry for the delay"

More principles (write any 2–3 as bonus)

  • Attention: listen/read fully; don't multitask while communicating.
  • Consistency: words match actions (don't say "be punctual" while coming late).
  • Timeliness: right info at the right time (exam date told early, not last night).
  • Feedback: always confirm understanding.
  • Proper channel: match medium to message (sensitive news face-to-face, not SMS).
Memory trick: "Can Cows Chew Cold Corn Cob Candy?" = Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous.

Topic 5: Basic Writing Skills

Why writing matters

Permanent record • formal/professional • reaches many people far away • can be re-read and checked. Examples: applications, emails, reports, notices.

(a) The sentence — foundation of writing

A sentence needs Subject (who) + Predicate (what about them): "Amit (S) plays football (P)." Must start with CAPITAL letter, end with full stop ./ ? / ! 4 types: Declarative (statement — "I study IT."), Interrogative (question — "Where is the file?"), Imperative (order/request — "Open page 10." / "Please sit."), Exclamatory (feeling — "What a goal!").

(b) Parts of speech — quick table (NCERT favourite)

PartJobExample
NounName (person/place/thing)Amit, Delhi, book
PronounReplaces nounhe, she, it, they
VerbAction/staterun, is, write
AdjectiveDescribes nountall boy, red pen
AdverbDescribes verb/adj.runs fast, very tall
PrepositionShows relation (in/on/at)book on table
ConjunctionJoins words/sentencesand, but, because
InterjectionSudden feelingWow! Oh! Alas!

(c) Paragraph writing (3 parts)

  1. Topic sentence — main idea ("Yoga keeps students healthy.").
  2. Supporting sentences — 3–4 details/examples.
  3. Concluding sentence — ending line ("So every student should do yoga daily.").

(d) Articles + Phrases

Articles: A (consonant sound — a book) • An (vowel sound — an apple, an honest boy) • The (unique/specific — the Sun, the Taj Mahal). Phrase = group of words with NO complete meaning (no subject+verb), e.g. "in the morning", "on the table" — phrases BUILD sentences.

(e) Punctuation + Capitalization rules

  • Capital: sentence start, names (Amit), places (Delhi), days/months (Monday, June), "I", languages.
  • Full stop (.) ends statements • comma (,) separates items/pauses • (?) questions • (!) feelings • (') apostrophe for missing letters (don't) • (:) colon before lists.

Writing Do's and Don'ts

✅ Do❌ Don't
Plan points before writingStart without thinking
Short, clear sentencesLong confusing lines
One idea per paragraphMix many ideas together
Check grammar + spellingSend first draft
Polite formal wordsSlang (hey, yaar) in formal writing

Exam Q&A — Unit 1

1-markers (with answers):

  1. Name the 3 methods of communication. → Verbal, non-verbal, visual.
  2. Phone call is which type of verbal communication? → Oral.
  3. Tone of voice in non-verbal study is called? → Paralanguage (tone/pitch).
  4. Last step of the communication cycle? → Feedback.
  5. "French manual" barrier type? → Linguistic barrier.
  6. 👍 understood differently in countries = which barrier? → Cultural.
  7. Expand one C: message with facts/figures ("20%"). → Concrete.
  8. "Submit file by Friday 5 pm" shows which C? → Clear.
  9. "Wow!" is which part of speech? → Interjection.
  10. First line of a paragraph is called? → Topic sentence.

2-markers (model answers, 20–30 words):

  1. Explain oral and written communication with one advantage each. → Oral = spoken (phone, speech); instant feedback + fast. Written = letters/emails; permanent record + can be re-read.
  2. Why is feedback significant? (2 points) → (1) Confirms the message was understood correctly. (2) Corrects errors early and improves future performance.
  3. Suggest two measures to overcome language barriers. → (1) Use simple common words + short sentences, explain jargon. (2) Use visuals/translator and confirm with feedback.
  4. Explain any two Cs with examples. → (1) Clear — simple exact message ("Submit by Friday 5 pm"). (2) Courteous — polite words ("please", "thank you").
  5. Write two rules of good paragraph writing. → (1) Start with a topic sentence stating the main idea. (2) Add 3–4 supporting details + one concluding line; one idea per paragraph.
