Part 1 — Your Book's Questions (Page 11) 5 questions

BOOK • Q.a

a. Which of the following are the two major components of communication?

Answer: ii. Communicator and audience
Why: every communication needs a sender (communicator) who sends and a receiver (audience) who receives — these two ends are its major components.
BOOK • Q.b

b. Which of the following is the simplest form of communication?

Answer: ii. Non-verbal communication
Why: gestures, expressions and tone need no language or literacy — even babies use them, so it is the most basic/simplest form.
BOOK • Q.c

c. Your school is organising a festival and you have to inform the people of your locality about it. Which form of communication would be the most suitable?

Answer: iv. Both i. and iii.
Why: posters/banners (visual) + announcements/word-of-mouth (verbal) together reach the whole locality fastest.
BOOK • Q.d

d. Which of the following is a planned communication used to convey ideas, programs, thoughts, presentations, data etc., to the masses, students or audiences?

Answer: iv. Public communication
Why: public communication = one speaker addresses a large audience (assembly speech, seminar). Interpersonal = between 2 people; intrapersonal = with yourself; small group = team discussion.
BOOK • Q.e

e. Which of the following communication is most suitable to advertise a product?

Answer: iv. Visual communication
Why: posters, hoardings, banners and video ads attract the eye instantly and cross language barriers — ideal for advertising.

Part 2 — 15 Extra Practice MCQs (Unit 1) 15 questions

EXTRA • Q6

6. In the communication cycle, ENCODING is done by the ___.

Answer: ii. Sender
Why: the sender encodes (converts thought into words/gestures); the receiver decodes (interprets it).
EXTRA • Q7

7. Feedback flows ___.

Answer: ii. From receiver to sender
Why: feedback is the receiver's response flowing BACK — it completes the loop.
EXTRA • Q8

8. "Slide 3 is wrong — recheck the figures and use a bigger font." This is ___ feedback.

Answer: iii. Constructive
Why: it points out the error AND gives a solution — that combination is constructive (the best type).
EXTRA • Q9

9. A Hindi-speaking worker receives a French manual. This is a ___ barrier.

Answer: ii. Linguistic
Why: different languages / tough jargon = language barrier. Fix: simple words, visuals, translator.
EXTRA • Q10

10. "Sales rose 20% this month." This message shows which C?

Answer: iii. Concrete
Why: concrete = specific facts and figures (20%), not vague words like "a lot".
EXTRA • Q11

11. The first line of a paragraph, stating the main idea, is the ___.

Answer: ii. Topic sentence
Why: paragraph order = topic sentence → supporting details → concluding line.
EXTRA • Q12

12. In "Wow! What a goal!", the word "Wow" is a/an ___.

Answer: iv. Interjection
Why: interjections express sudden feeling (Wow! Oh! Alas!) and end with "!"
EXTRA • Q13

13. Which of these is an IMPERATIVE sentence?

Answer: iii. "Please open page 10."
Why: imperative = order/request. (i) declarative, (ii) interrogative, (iv) exclamatory.
EXTRA • Q14

14. Which method of communication gives a permanent record?

Answer: ii. Written
Why: letters, emails and reports stay as proof and can be re-read; speech vanishes.
EXTRA • Q15

15. A traffic signal is an example of ___ communication.

Answer: iii. Visual
Why: signs, signals, charts and symbols that you SEE = visual communication.
EXTRA • Q16

16. "Submit the file by Friday 5 pm" is an example of which C?

Answer: i. Clear
Why: exact, simple, one-idea message with no confusion = clarity (vs vague "do it soon").
EXTRA • Q17

17. A thumbs-up sign is praised in one country but rude in another. This is a ___ barrier.

Answer: iii. Cultural
Why: different customs and gesture-meanings across cultures = cultural barrier.
EXTRA • Q18

18. Which of these is NOT one of the 7 Cs?

Answer: iii. Colourful
Why: the 7 Cs are Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous — "Colourful" is the odd one.
EXTRA • Q19

19. Thinking, self-talk and reflection is called ___ communication.

Answer: ii. Intrapersonal
Why: intra = within → communication WITH YOURSELF. Inter = between → with another person.
EXTRA • Q20

20. A worker cannot reach the manager because of strict company levels. This is a/an ___ barrier.

Answer: iii. Organisational
Why: rigid hierarchy/rules blocking message flow = organisational barrier. Fix: open-door policy, suggestion boxes.

Unit 1+ — 20 More Tricky MCQs (Q21–Q40) 20 questions

U1+ • Q21

21. In the communication cycle, DECODING is done by the ___.

Answer: iii. Receiver
Why: sender ENCODES (makes message), receiver DECODES (interprets it).
U1+ • Q22

22. Which of these is NOT verbal communication?

Answer: iii. Smile
Why: a smile uses no words — it is non-verbal; the rest use words.
U1+ • Q23

23. Paralanguage refers to ___.

Answer: ii. Tone, pitch and speed of voice
Why: paralanguage = HOW you say it (tone/pitch/speed), a part of non-verbal communication.
U1+ • Q24

24. "You spoke for 12 minutes with 10 slides." This feedback is ___.

Answer: iii. Descriptive
Why: it states only facts with no praise, blame or advice = descriptive.
U1+ • Q25

25. Saying only "Good job!" without details is ___ feedback.

Answer: ii. Vague / general
Why: it names no exact point. Specific version: "Your introduction was clear."
U1+ • Q26

26. Getting your exam result after a month is ___ feedback.

Answer: ii. Delayed
Why: it arrives long after the act. Immediate feedback = instant, like a nod in class.
U1+ • Q27

27. A student nodding while the teacher explains is ___.

Answer: ii. Immediate non-verbal feedback
Why: a nod is instant (immediate) and uses no words (non-verbal).
U1+ • Q28

28. "Meeting at 10." with no extra words shows which C?

Answer: i. Concise
Why: concise = short, to the point, no extra words.
U1+ • Q29

29. An invite with date + time + venue + contact number shows which C?

Answer: ii. Complete
Why: complete = all needed info (what, when, where, whom) included.
U1+ • Q30

30. Using "please", "thank you" and "sorry for the delay" shows which C?

Answer: iv. Courteous
Why: courteous = polite and respectful language.
U1+ • Q31

31. Writing "First… Next… Finally…" in logical order shows which C?

Answer: iii. Coherent
Why: coherent = ideas linked in logical order, no random jumps.
U1+ • Q32

32. Checking dates, names and grammar before sending shows which C?

Answer: iv. Correct
Why: correct = accurate facts, grammar and spellings.
U1+ • Q33

33. A patient cannot understand the doctor's Latin terms. This is a ___ barrier.

Answer: ii. Linguistic
Why: tough jargon/terms the listener doesn't know = language barrier.
U1+ • Q34

34. Loud construction noise disturbing an online class is a ___ barrier.

Answer: i. Physical
Why: environment disturbances (noise, distance, bad network) = physical barrier.
U1+ • Q35

35. An angry student cannot hear the teacher's advice. This is a ___ barrier.

Answer: iv. Emotional
Why: anger, fear or stress blocking listening = emotional barrier.
U1+ • Q36

36. "He is always late" — judging before listening is a ___ barrier.

Answer: iii. Perceptual
Why: fixed viewpoints/assumptions about people = perceptual barrier.
U1+ • Q37

37. No suggestion box and workers cannot reach the boss. This is a ___ barrier.

Answer: iii. Organisational
Why: rigid structure/rules blocking message flow = organisational barrier.
U1+ • Q38

38. Saying "So you mean…?" to confirm understanding shows ___.

Answer: ii. Active listening
Why: paraphrasing + eye contact + no interruption = active listening.
U1+ • Q39

39. Which of these is an EXCLAMATORY sentence?

Answer: iii. "What a beautiful garden!"
Why: exclamatory = strong feeling + "!". Others: declarative, interrogative, imperative.
U1+ • Q40

40. In "She runs very fast", the word "very" is a/an ___.

Answer: ii. Adverb
Why: "very" describes "fast" (an adverb/verb quality) — words describing verbs/adjectives are adverbs.

Feedback &amp; Cycle Drill-2 (Q61–Q80) 20 questions

F2 • Q61

Answer: ii. Sender
Why: cycle starts with sender; ends with feedback.
F2 • Q62

62. The message travels through the ___.

Answer: iii. Channel / Medium

F2 • Q63

63. The receiver converting the message into meaning is ___.

Answer: ii. Decoding
Why: sender encodes, receiver decodes.
F2 • Q64

64. Without feedback, communication remains ___.

Answer: ii. One-way
Why: only feedback makes it two-way/complete.
F2 • Q65

65. "Well done, team!" is ___ feedback.

Answer: i. Positive
Why: praise/appreciation = positive.
F2 • Q66

66. "Your report has 3 spelling errors" (no solution given) is ___.

Answer: ii. Negative / critical
Why: error with NO fix = critical; WITH fix = constructive.
F2 • Q67

67. "Wrong total — re-add column B" is ___ feedback.

Answer: iii. Constructive
Why: error + exact fix = constructive (best type).
F2 • Q68

68. Which feedback type is MOST useful?

Answer: ii. Constructive
Why: it corrects AND teaches the fix.
F2 • Q69

F2 • Q70

Answer: ii. Descriptive

F2 • Q72

Answer: i. Feedback
Why: inviting response = pulling feedback.
F2 • Q73

73. Which step CLOSES the communication loop?

Answer: iii. Feedback

F2 • Q74

F2 • Q77

F2 • Q78

Why: pinpoints the exact spot + need.
F2 • Q79

79. Honest feedback between people builds ___.

Answer: ii. Trust

F2 • Q80

Why: check receipt AND raise future quality.

7C • Q112

112. Jumping topics randomly violates ___.

Writing Skills Drill-2 (Q121–Q140) 20 questions

W2 • Q121

121. Every sentence MUST start with a ___.

Answer: ii. CAPITAL letter
Why: basic writing rule — capital start, ./ ? / ! end.
W2 • Q122

122. "Where are you?" is a ___ sentence.

Answer: ii. Interrogative
Why: asks a question + ends with "?"
W2 • Q123

123. "Sit down." is an ___ sentence.

Answer: iii. Imperative
Why: order/command = imperative.
W2 • Q124

124. "I love reading." is a ___ sentence.

Answer: i. Declarative
Why: plain statement + full stop = declarative.
W2 • Q125

125. A sentence = ___ + Predicate.

W2 • Q126

126. In "Delhi is big", "Delhi" is a ___.

Answer: ii. Noun
Why: name of a place = noun.
W2 • Q127

127. In "They play well", "They" is a ___.

Answer: ii. Pronoun
Why: replaces a noun (he/she/it/they).
W2 • Q128

128. In "She sings sweetly", "sweetly" is an ___.

Answer: iii. Adverb
Why: describes HOW she sings (verb) = adverb.
W2 • Q129

129. In "red pen", "red" is an ___.

Answer: ii. Adjective
Why: describes the noun (pen) = adjective.
W2 • Q130

130. In "book on the table", "on" is a ___.

Answer: ii. Preposition
Why: shows relation/position (in/on/at) = preposition.
W2 • Q131

131. "And / but / because" are ___.

Answer: ii. Conjunctions
Why: joining words = conjunctions.
W2 • Q132

132. "Alas! He lost." — "Alas" is an ___.

Answer: iv. Interjection
Why: sudden feeling + "!" = interjection.
W2 • Q133

133. The LAST line of a paragraph is the ___.

Answer: iii. Concluding sentence
Why: wraps up the main idea at the end.
W2 • Q134

134. The MIDDLE lines of a paragraph are ___.

Answer: ii. Supporting
Why: details/examples supporting the topic sentence.
W2 • Q135

135. "___ apple a day keeps the doctor away." Fill the article.

Answer: ii. An Why: "An" before vowel SOUNDS (apple, honest); "A" before consonant sounds; "The" for unique/specific things (the Sun).
W2 • Q136

136. Which of these is a PHRASE (not a full sentence)?

Answer: i. "in the morning" Why: phrase = words with NO complete meaning (no subject+verb); the rest are complete sentences.
W2 • Q137

137. In "The little girl laughed loudly", the PREDICATE is ___.

Answer: ii. "laughed loudly" Why: subject (who) = The little girl; predicate (what about them — verb + rest) = laughed loudly.
W2 • Q138

138. "Don't" uses an ___.

Answer: ii. Apostrophe
Why: apostrophe (') marks the missing letter (do not → don't).
W2 • Q139

139. Always capitalise ___.

Answer: ii. Names, places, days
Why: proper nouns + sentence starts + "I" take capitals.
W2 • Q140

140. A good paragraph sticks to ___ main idea.

Answer: i. One Why: one idea per paragraph (unity); start a new paragraph for a new idea.
