## SHORT ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS (15)

**S1. ⭐ Enlist the different methods of communication.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Verbal** — oral (speech, phone, meeting) + written (letter, email, report, notice). (2) **Non-verbal** — expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, tone of voice. (3) **Visual** — charts, maps, signals, posters, videos.

**S2. ⭐ Which is the easiest method of communication and why?**
> **Ans:** **Non-verbal** is the easiest — it needs no language or literacy. Gestures, expressions and tone are understood naturally (even babies use them) and work across different languages.

**S3. ⭐ Differentiate between verbal and non-verbal communication.**
> **Ans:** **Verbal** uses words (speech, email) — precise and recordable. **Non-verbal** uses no words (smile, gesture, tone) — instant and emotional. Best communication combines both: words carry facts, body carries feelings.

**S4. ⭐ Explain in your own words how body language affects our communication.**
> **Ans:** Body language (expressions, posture, eye contact) carries most of the message's impact and shows true feelings. Eye contact + open posture build trust; crossed arms + no eye contact create distrust — receivers believe the body over words.

**S5. ⭐ What do you understand by visual communication? Give a few examples where it is used.**
> **Ans:** Visual communication conveys meaning through pictures/signs instead of words. Examples: traffic signals, road maps, pie charts in reports, no-smoking signs, fest posters, company logos. It is fast and crosses language barriers.

**S6. What is feedback? Why is it called the lifeline of communication?**
> **Ans:** Feedback is the receiver's response flowing back to the sender. It is the lifeline because it (1) confirms the message was understood and (2) corrects errors early — completing the two-way loop.

**S7. Name any two barriers to communication with one measure to overcome each.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Linguistic** (French manual) → use simple words + visuals/translator. (2) **Physical** (classroom noise) → shift to a quiet room with a working mic.

**S8. Explain any two Cs of effective communication with examples.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Clear** — simple, exact message ("Submit the file by Friday 5 pm"). (2) **Courteous** — polite words ("please", "thank you", "sorry for the delay").

**S9. What is paralanguage? Give two examples.**
> **Ans:** Paralanguage is HOW the voice sounds — tone, pitch, speed, pauses (part of non-verbal). Examples: loud-fast voice shows excitement/anger; soft-slow voice shows calmness/sadness. **Book fact:** only 7% words — 38% voice + 55% body!

**S10. Differentiate encoding and decoding.**
> **Ans:** **Encoding** (sender's job) = converting a thought into words/gestures. **Decoding** (receiver's job) = interpreting the received message into meaning.

**S11. What is a topic sentence? Why is it important?**
> **Ans:** The topic sentence is the first line stating the paragraph's main idea (e.g. "Yoga keeps students healthy."). It guides the reader and keeps the writer's supporting lines on track.

**S12. In "Wow! What a goal!", identify the part of speech of "Wow" and the sentence type.**
> **Ans:** "Wow" is an **interjection** (sudden feeling + "!"). "What a goal!" is an **exclamatory** sentence.

**S13. State any two advantages of written communication.**
> **Ans:** (1) It creates a **permanent record/proof** that can be re-read and checked. (2) It is **formal** and reaches far-off people with the exact same message.

**S14. What is constructive feedback? Give one example.**
> **Ans:** Constructive feedback points out an error AND suggests a solution — the best type. Example: "Slide 3 is wrong — recheck the figures and use a bigger font."

**S15. What are articles? Fill in: (a) ___ honest boy (b) ___ Sun rises in the East.**
> **Ans:** Articles (a/an/the) point to nouns. (a) **An** honest boy — "An" before vowel SOUNDS (h is silent). (b) **The** Sun — "The" for unique/single objects. Rule: A = consonant sound (a book), An = vowel sound (an apple), The = specific/unique (the Taj Mahal).

## LONG ANSWER TYPE QUESTIONS (12)

**L1. ⭐ Discuss in detail the verbal method of communication.**
> **Ans:** Verbal = communication using WORDS, in two forms. **Oral** (speech, phone, meeting, video call): merits — fast, instant feedback, personal touch, doubts cleared at once; demerits — no record, forgotten easily, weak for long data. **Written** (letter, email, report, notice): merits — permanent proof, re-readable, formal, same message to all; demerits — slow, no instant feedback, needs literacy. Use oral for urgent/personal matters, written for formal/record needs.

**L2. ⭐ Explain the various elements of non-verbal communication.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Facial expressions** — smile/frown show feelings instantly. (2) **Gestures** — wave, thumbs-up, pointing. (3) **Posture** — straight = confident, slouched = careless. (4) **Eye contact** — shows honesty and attention. (5) **Touch** — handshake, pat. (6) **Paralanguage** — tone, pitch, speed of voice. (7) **Appearance/space** — dress and distance. Together they carry most impact — receivers trust the body over words.

**L3. ⭐ What do you understand by visual communication? Enlist its advantages and disadvantages.**
> **Ans:** Visual = meaning through pictures/signs (signals, maps, charts, posters, logos, videos). **Advantages:** very fast to grasp; understood across languages; attractive and memorable — ideal for ads and teaching. **Disadvantages:** needs design skill/time; cannot express complex detail alone; may be misread without context; printing/display costs. **Uses:** traffic control, classrooms (charts), business reports (graphs), advertising (hoardings).

**L4. ⭐ Compare verbal and non-verbal communication.**
> **Ans:** **Medium:** words vs body/tone. **Record:** written-verbal is permanent; non-verbal vanishes. **Feedback:** oral gives instant reply; non-verbal is read instantly too. **Literacy:** written needs reading skill; non-verbal needs none. **Emotion:** non-verbal shows true feelings; words can hide them. **Example:** "I'm fine" in an angry tone — words say fine, body says upset; wise listeners trust the body.

**L5. Explain the communication cycle with an example.**
> **Ans:** The 7-step loop: **Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback** (feedback arrow returns to sender). Example: teacher explains photosynthesis (sender/encoding) → speaks in class (message/channel) → student understands (decoding) → student nods/asks a doubt (feedback). If feedback shows confusion, the sender re-explains — the loop continues till understanding is confirmed.

**L6. Discuss the significance of feedback and the qualities of good feedback.**
> **Ans:** **Significance (6):** confirms understanding; corrects errors early; completes one-way talk into two-way communication; improves performance; builds trust and relations; helps the sender improve. **Good feedback is:** specific (exact point), timely (soon), descriptive (facts, no insults), constructive (suggest a fix), polite, and about the WORK not the person ("the report needs dates", not "you are careless"). Supportive feedback also encourages and offers continuing help.

**L7. Explain any four barriers to communication with examples and measures to overcome them.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Physical** — class noise → quiet room, working mic. (2) **Linguistic** — French manual → simple words + visuals. (3) **Cultural** — thumbs-up rude abroad → learn customs. (4) **Emotional** — anger blocks listening → stay calm, right time. Plus two general rules: always listen actively with feedback, and summarise key points at the end. **Book note:** the CBSE book groups emotional + perceptual factors under **Interpersonal** barriers (tone, emotions, personal biases).

**L8. Explain the 7 Cs of effective communication with one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Clear** — "Submit file by Friday 5 pm". **Concise** — "Meeting at 10". **Concrete** — "Sales rose 20%". **Correct** — checked dates/grammar. **Coherent** — First→Next→Finally order. **Complete** — invite with date+time+venue+contact. **Courteous** — "please/thank you". Trick: "Can Cows Chew Cold Corn Cob Candy?"

**L9. Describe basic writing skills: sentences, phrases and paragraphs.**
> **Ans:** **Sentence** = Subject + Predicate, CAPITAL start, ./ ? / ! end; 4 kinds — declarative, interrogative, imperative, exclamatory. **Phrase** = group of words with NO complete meaning (no subject+verb), e.g. "in the morning" — phrases BUILD sentences. **Paragraph** = topic sentence → 3–4 supporting lines → concluding line; one idea each. Add articles (a/an/the), capitals, punctuation and logical order.

**L10. What is active listening? Give six do's of a good listener.**
> **Ans:** Active listening = fully concentrating to understand the speaker and respond thoughtfully (not just hearing). Six do's: (1) face the speaker with eye contact; (2) never interrupt; (3) nod to show attention; (4) paraphrase — "So you mean…?"; (5) ask clarifying questions; (6) note key points. It prevents perceptual barriers and builds trust.

**L11. How do you choose the right method of communication? Explain with four situations.**
> **Ans:** Decide by urgency, distance, audience, feelings involved and whether a record is needed. (1) Urgent doubt nearby → **oral** (instant). (2) Leave application → **written** (proof). (3) Upset friend → **face-to-face** (tone + care). (4) Stranger, unknown language → **visual** (map/signs). Combining methods (report + chart + talk) works best for important messages.

**L12. Explain the 9 parts of speech (5 basic + 4 supporting) with one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Basic 5:** Noun (Amit, book) • Pronoun (he, they) • Verb (run, write) • Adjective (tall boy) • Adverb (runs fast). **Supporting 4:** Article (a, an, the — points to nouns) • Preposition (book on table) • Conjunction (and, because) • Interjection (Wow!). Article picks which noun: **a** boy (any) vs **the** boy (that one). Sample: "Wow! (interjection) Amit (noun) runs (verb) very (adverb) fast (adverb) and (conjunction) wins." Correct usage = correct (C) writing.

## APPLICATION BASED QUESTIONS (12)

**A1. ⭐ When you see an e-mail full of grammatical errors or incomplete sentences, how do you react? Does it affect your perception of the sender? Why or why not?**
> **Ans:** I struggle to understand it and my respect for the sender drops — errors signal carelessness and break the **Correct** and **Clear** principles. But I stay fair: if the sender is a non-native speaker, I focus on the content and politely ask for clarity instead of judging.

**A2. ⭐ How aware are you of your own body language? Has your body language ever got you in trouble while communicating with someone?**
> **Ans:** I now watch my habits — eye contact, no fidgeting, open posture. Once in a viva I sat cross-armed (I was only nervous) and the teacher thought me rude — my marks suffered. Lesson learnt: the body speaks first, so I keep a smile, straight posture and calm hands.

**A3. ⭐ You are the CEO of a company. Which type of communication would you choose to convey the rules and regulations to the employees and why?**
> **Ans:** **Written** (official circular + email + notice board) — rules need identical wording for all, permanent proof and re-reading. I would follow it with a short **oral** town-hall to explain and clear doubts. Written = authority + record; oral = understanding.

**A4. ⭐ Imagine you have to justify that Virat Kohli is a better captain than MS Dhoni to a group of audience. Which method of communication will be most appropriate for this justification and why?**
> **Ans:** **Oral public communication** (live speech/debate) supported by **visuals** (win-% charts, chase records on screen). Persuasion needs voice modulation, gestures, eye contact and instant Q&A — plus data visuals for proof. Written text alone cannot win over a live crowd.

**A5. Riya messages her team "do it soon". Identify the violated C and rewrite the message correctly.**
> **Ans:** Violates **Clear** (vague, no deadline) and **Complete** (no what/when). Rewrite: *"Please submit the sales sheet by Friday 5 pm on email."* — now clear, complete and courteous.

**A6. A team leader's juniors look confused by his technical jargon. What two steps should he take?**
> **Ans:** (1) Replace jargon with simple words + show a chart/demo (attacks the linguistic barrier). (2) Pause, ask "any doubts?", and paraphrase replies to confirm (feedback + active listening).

**A7. For the school fest, Class X puts up posters, announces on loudspeaker, and sends a written notice to parents. Name the 3 methods and explain why combining beats using one.**
> **Ans:** Posters = **visual**; loudspeaker = **oral**; notice = **written**. Combination wins: visual attracts, oral spreads fast, written is proof — together covering busy, far-off and record-needing audiences.

**A8. Your best friend is upset after failing a test. Which method of communication will you choose and why?**
> **Ans:** **Face-to-face oral + non-verbal** — sit together, listen patiently, keep a caring tone and eye contact. Feelings need warmth and instant response; a cold text message cannot console anyone.

**A9. You must present monthly sales to your manager. What combination will you use and why?**
> **Ans:** **Written report + visual charts**, with a short **oral** summary in the meeting. Report = permanent record for the file; charts = one-glance understanding; oral talk = doubts cleared instantly.

**A10. A stranger speaking an unknown language asks you for directions. What will you do?**
> **Ans:** Switch to **visual + gestures**: draw a simple map, point left/right, show fingers for distance, or use a translator/map app. Speak slowly with a smile — patience plus pictures beats the language barrier.

**A11. Your friend says "I'm fine" in an angry tone with crossed arms. What do you believe and what will you do?**
> **Ans:** I believe the **body, not the words** — tone + crossed arms contradict "fine" (non-verbal carries true feelings). I will gently say, "You seem upset — want to talk?" and listen without forcing.

**A12. A school trip notice misses the date and venue. Which C is violated? Rewrite the notice correctly.**
> **Ans:** Violates **Complete** (missing when/where) and **Clear**. Rewrite: *"Trip to Lotus Temple on Tue 16 Dec. Bus leaves 8 am, returns 4 pm. Submit consent + Rs. 200 by Friday. — Class Teacher."*

## COMPETENCY BASED QUESTIONS (12)

**C1. ⭐ Ravi normally shares his thoughts with Ram face-to-face, and they discuss and reply to each other. Which form is this — (i) Interpersonal or (ii) Written? Why?** *(Photo was cut; answered as read.)*
> **Ans:** **(i) Interpersonal Communication** — it happens between two people, face-to-face, with instant two-way exchange (talk + reply + body language). Written would be one-way and delayed, with no discussion.

**C2. Amit presents with zero eye contact, 40 text-packed slides in 10 minutes, and says "no questions, I am getting late". The manager understands nothing. (a) Identify 4 mistakes. (b) Suggest 4 fixes.**
> **Ans: (a)** No eye contact (poor non-verbal) • overloaded slides (not concise) • too fast (not clear) • no questions (blocks feedback. **(b)** Eye contact + open posture • max 10 slides, 1 idea each • slow clear speech • invite questions + summarise at end.

**C3. In a job interview, a candidate gives no eye contact, sits slouched, and mumbles answers. (a) Identify 3 non-verbal problems and the impression each creates. (b) Give 3 fixes.**
> **Ans: (a)** No eye contact → seems dishonest/nervous • slouching → seems careless • mumbling → seems unprepared. **(b)** Steady eye contact + smile • straight, open posture • clear, slow speech with prepared short answers.

**C4. Two teams advertise a blood-donation camp: Team A only chats about it in class; Team B puts up posters, announces in assembly, and sends a parent circular. (a) Which team communicates better? (b) Name Team B's 3 methods and one strength of each.**
> **Ans: (a)** **Team B** — wider reach + record. **(b)** Posters = **visual** (attracts, memorable) • assembly = **oral** (fast, whole school at once) • circular = **written** (proof for parents). Team A's oral-only chat reaches few and is forgotten.

**C5. A teacher compares two "My Aim" answers — A: one 20-line unbroken block; B: 3 short paragraphs, each starting with a clear point. (a) Whose answer is better? (b) List 4 paragraph rules B followed.**
> **Ans: (a)** **B** — broken into readable idea-chunks. **(b)** One idea per paragraph • topic sentence first • 3–4 supporting lines • concluding line (+ capitals + full stops). A's wall of text hides ideas; B's structure shows them.

**C6. In a project team, members keep interrupting, assume meanings without asking, and never review each other's work. (a) Name 3 barriers/skills violated. (b) Give a 3-point fix.**
> **Ans: (a)** **Interpersonal** (interruptions) • **Perceptual** (assumptions) • **No feedback** (no review). **(b)** Turn-taking rule + listen fully • paraphrase before replying ("So you mean…?") • weekly written minutes + peer review of drafts.

**C7. A teacher speaks very fast, uses no examples or visuals, and allows no doubts in class. Students fail the test. (a) Identify 3 teaching-communication mistakes. (b) Suggest 3 fixes.**
> **Ans: (a)** Too fast (not clear) • no visuals/examples (tough ideas stay abstract) • no questions (feedback loop cut). **(b)** Slow pace + daily-life examples • charts/demo/models • doubt breaks every 10 minutes + end-of-class recap.

**C8. Advertise (a) a village fair and (b) an online gadget. For EACH, choose the best method-combo and justify in one line.**
> **Ans: (a) Village fair:** loudspeaker announcements (oral) + painted banners (visual) — reaches low-literacy crowds fast and cheap. **(b) Online gadget:** demo video + infographics (visual) + written reviews — buyers compare specs visually before paying. Rule: audience decides the method.

**C9. You must convey sad news (a pet's death) to your little cousin. Options: text message, phone call, or going in person. Choose one and justify with 3 reasons.**
> **Ans:** **Go in person** (face-to-face). (1) Sensitive news needs caring tone + expressions (non-verbal). (2) You can hug, console and answer questions instantly. (3) Text/phone hide feelings and feel cold — the proper channel for emotional news is always personal.

**C10. In a group discussion, one student dominates, one stays silent, and two argue without listening. Suggest 3 rules to make this GD effective.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Listen, then speak** — no interrupting; build on others' points. (2) **Invite all** — "Sara, what's your view?" + 1-minute turns so none dominates. (3) **Respect + summarise** — disagree politely with reasons; one member recaps decisions at the end.

**C11. The hostel warden must announce an urgent midnight water-cut for tomorrow. Options: notice board, WhatsApp broadcast, morning assembly announcement. Choose the best combo and justify.**
> **Ans:** **WhatsApp broadcast + notice board + warden announcement combined.** Broadcast = instant reach tonight (speed); board = written proof nobody can deny; announcement = authority + doubts cleared. Urgent + important news always deserves more than one channel.

**C12. During exams, the invigilator only uses gestures — finger on lips for silence, pointing to seats, showing the clock for time. (a) Identify the method. (b) Give 2 merits of it HERE and 1 limitation.**
> **Ans: (a)** **Non-verbal** communication. **(b) Merits:** fully silent (zero disturbance) + instantly understood by all (universal gestures). **Limitation:** cannot explain complex rules (e.g. new marking scheme) — those still need clear written/oral instructions.

## SHORT ANSWER — SET 2 (S16–S45)

**S16. What are the two types of verbal communication? Give one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Oral** — spoken (e.g. classroom lecture). **Written** — reading/writing (e.g. email to principal).

**S17. Give two examples each of non-verbal and visual communication.**
> **Ans:** **Non-verbal:** handshake, thumbs-up. **Visual:** traffic signal, pie chart.

**S18. What is the difference between hearing and active listening?**
> **Ans:** **Hearing** = automatic sound detection. **Active listening** = fully concentrating to understand + respond (eye contact, no interruption, paraphrasing).

**S19. What is a channel/medium? Give three examples.**
> **Ans:** The path carrying the message from sender to receiver. Examples: air (speech), phone call, email.

**S20. Why is written communication called "permanent"? Give one limitation.**
> **Ans:** It stays as **proof** that can be re-read anytime (letters, records). **Limitation:** slow, with no instant feedback.

**S21. What is intrapersonal communication? Give one example.**
> **Ans:** Communication WITH YOURSELF — thinking, self-talk, reflection. Example: mentally rehearsing answers before a viva.

**S22. What is public communication? Give one example.**
> **Ans:** One speaker addressing a LARGE audience in planned form. Example: principal's speech in morning assembly.

**S23. List any two merits of oral communication.**
> **Ans:** (1) Fast with **instant feedback** and doubt-clearing. (2) Personal touch — tone + expressions add meaning.

**S24. List any two demerits of oral communication.**
> **Ans:** (1) **No permanent record** — forgotten or denied later. (2) Poor for long/complex data (numbers, rules get distorted).

**S25. What is decoding? Who performs it?**
> **Ans:** Decoding = interpreting the received message into meaning. It is performed by the **receiver** (sender does encoding).

**S26. Give one example each of positive and negative feedback.**
> **Ans:** **Positive:** "Excellent chart, well done!" **Negative:** "Your file has 5 spelling mistakes." (Add a fix to make it constructive.)

**S27. What is specific feedback? Convert "Nice work" into specific feedback.**
> **Ans:** Specific feedback names the EXACT point. Conversion: "Nice work" → *"Your introduction explains the topic clearly with one example."*

**S28. What is delayed feedback? Give one example.**
> **Ans:** Response arriving LONG after the act. Example: board exam result after two months (vs a nod in class = immediate).

**S29. Mention two qualities of good feedback.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Specific + timely** — exact point, given soon. (2) **Constructive + polite** — suggests a fix, targets work not person.

**S30. Why should feedback target work, not the person? Give one example.**
> **Ans:** Personal attacks hurt and block learning; work-focused feedback corrects without insult. Say *"The report needs dates"* — not *"You are careless."*

**S31. What is a physical barrier? Give two examples.**
> **Ans:** Environment/medium defects blocking the message. Examples: construction noise during online class; tiny font in dim light on a notice.

**S32. What is a cultural barrier? Give one example + one fix.**
> **Ans:** Different customs/gesture-meanings across cultures. Example: thumbs-up praised here, rude abroad. Fix: learn and respect the other's customs.

**S33. What is an emotional barrier? How can it be controlled?**
> **Ans:** Feelings (anger, fear, stress) blocking listening — e.g. an angry student hears no advice. Control: calm down first, choose the right time, listen patiently.

**S34. What is an organisational barrier? Suggest one fix.**
> **Ans:** Company structure blocking message flow — e.g. worker cannot reach manager through levels. Fix: open-door policy + suggestion boxes + regular meetings.

**S35. What is meant by "Clear" and "Concise"? Give one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Clear** = exact, confusion-free ("Submit by Friday 5 pm"). **Concise** = short, no extras ("Meeting at 10").

**S36. What is meant by "Concrete" and "Complete"? Give one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Concrete** = facts/figures ("Sales rose 20%"). **Complete** = all needed info ("Trip Tue 16 Dec, 8 am–4 pm, Rs. 200 by Friday").

**S37. What is coherence in writing? Give one bad + one good example.**
> **Ans:** Coherence = ideas in logical linked order. Bad: "Fees… picnic… homework… bye." Good: "First pay fees by Friday. Next, submit the trip form. Finally, revise Ch-2."

**S38. Why is courtesy important? Give two magic words/sentences.**
> **Ans:** Polite words earn cooperation; rude words build resistance even when you are right. Magic: *"Please submit by Friday"* and *"Thank you for your help."*

**S39. What are imperative sentences? Give two examples.**
> **Ans:** Sentences giving orders/requests. Examples: "Open page 10." / "Please sit down."

**S40. What are interrogative sentences? Give two examples.**
> **Ans:** Sentences asking questions, ending with "?". Examples: "Where is the file?" / "Did you finish homework?"

**S41. Identify the parts of speech: "quickly" and "beautiful". Use each in a phrase.**
> **Ans:** "quickly" = **adverb** ("runs quickly"). "beautiful" = **adjective** ("beautiful garden"). Adverbs describe verbs; adjectives describe nouns.

**S42. What is a conjunction? Join using one: "It rained. We played."**
> **Ans:** Conjunctions join words/sentences (and, but, because). Joined: *"It rained, **but** we played."*

**S43. What is a preposition? Fill in: "The book is ___ the table."**
> **Ans:** Prepositions show relation/position (in, on, at, under). Answer: *"The book is **on** the table."*

**S44. What is a phrase? Give two examples. How is it different from a sentence?**
> **Ans:** A phrase = group of words with NO complete meaning (no subject+verb): "in the morning", "on the table". A sentence = complete meaning with subject + predicate ("Birds fly in the morning."). Phrases are sentence building-blocks.

**S45. State two capitalization rules with examples.**
> **Ans:** (1) Start every sentence with a capital: *"We won."* (2) Capitalise names/places/days/"I": *"Amit, Delhi, Monday, I".*

## LONG ANSWER — SET 2 (L13–L32)

**L13. Explain oral communication: meaning, examples, merits, demerits and best uses.**
> **Ans:** Oral = spoken words (talk, phone, speech, meeting, video call). **Merits:** fast, instant feedback, personal, doubts cleared live, body language adds meaning. **Demerits:** no record, forgotten/distorted, poor for numbers and legal proof. **Best uses:** urgent matters, discussions, counselling, persuasion, quick coordination.

**L14. Explain written communication: meaning, examples, merits, demerits and best uses.**
> **Ans:** Written = reading/writing (letter, email, report, notice, books, SMS). **Merits:** permanent proof, re-readable, identical message to all, formal, works over distance/time. **Demerits:** slow, no instant feedback, needs literacy, tone can be misread. **Best uses:** rules, orders, applications, records, far-off formal messages.

**L15. Describe intrapersonal, interpersonal, small-group and public communication with one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Intrapersonal** — with self: rehearsing answers mentally. **Interpersonal** — between two: friend consoling friend. **Small-group** — 3–12 interacting: project team of 5 planning. **Public** — one speaker to masses: principal's assembly speech. Size grows: self → two → few → many.

**L16. Explain the communication cycle step-by-step with a classroom example.**
> **Ans:** (1) **Sender** (teacher) has an idea. (2) **Encoding:** converts it into simple words + diagram. (3) **Message:** the explanation itself. (4) **Channel:** speech + board. (5) **Decoding:** students interpret. (6) **Receiver:** the class. (7) **Feedback:** nods/doubts flow back; confused looks make the teacher re-explain — loop till all understand.

**L17. What are the types of feedback? Explain any four with examples.**
> **Ans:** **Positive:** "Great chart!" (praise). **Negative:** "Two totals are wrong." (error only). **Constructive:** "Totals wrong — re-add column B." (error + fix — best). **Descriptive:** "You spoke 5 minutes." (facts only). Others: specific vs vague, immediate (nod) vs delayed (result), formal (report card) vs informal (smile). Also **No feedback** (silence — itself signals disagreement).

**L18. Why is feedback called a two-way process? Explain with an example.**
> **Ans:** Message flows sender→receiver (way 1); feedback flows receiver→sender (way 2) — both sides send AND receive. Example: manager assigns a task (way 1) → employee asks "deadline Friday?" (way 2) → manager confirms (way 1 again). This to-and-fro continues till both agree — hence two-way.

**L19. Explain physical, linguistic and cultural barriers with one example + one fix each.**
> **Ans:** **Physical:** generator noise in class → quiet room + mic. **Linguistic:** English notice for Hindi-only parents → bilingual notice + pictures. **Cultural:** left-hand giving offends some guests → learn and respect local customs. Rule: match environment, language and manners to the audience.

**L20. Explain emotional, perceptual, organisational and interpersonal barriers with one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Emotional:** sobbing child hears no scolding. **Perceptual:** "contract staff are lazy" — judging before listening. **Organisational:** peon cannot reach director through 4 levels. **Interpersonal:** quarrelling partners stop talking. Fixes: right time, open mind, open-door policy, polite rebuilding of trust. **Book note:** the CBSE book files emotional + perceptual factors under **Interpersonal** barriers.

**L21. Describe 8 measures to overcome communication barriers.**
> **Ans:** ① Simple clear language for the audience. ② Active listening (eye contact, no interruption, "So you mean…?"). ③ Right medium (urgent→call, proof→email). ④ Remove disturbances (silence phone, quiet room). ⑤ Give + demand feedback. ⑥ Visuals for tough ideas. ⑦ Calm polite confident behaviour. ⑧ Repeat/summarise key points at the end.

**L22. Explain Clear, Concise and Concrete with examples — and what goes wrong if each is violated.**
> **Ans:** **Clear:** "Submit by Friday 5 pm" — vague "soon" causes missed deadlines. **Concise:** "Meeting at 10" — 3-page mail for it wastes time and hides the point. **Concrete:** "Rose 20%" — "rose a lot" convinces nobody and invites doubt. Together they make messages exact, quick and believable.

**L23. Explain Correct, Coherent, Complete and Courteous with one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Correct:** verified date/grammar ("Exam on 16 Dec", checked). **Coherent:** First pay fees → Next submit form → Finally revise. **Complete:** invite with date+time+venue+contact. **Courteous:** "Please send the file; thank you!" Correctness builds trust, order builds sense, completeness avoids follow-ups, courtesy earns cooperation.

**L24. Explain attention, consistency, timeliness and proper channel with one example each.**
> **Ans:** **Attention:** keep phone aside while the teacher explains. **Consistency:** practise the punctuality you preach. **Timeliness:** announce the trip a week early, not last night. **Proper channel:** console face-to-face, never by SMS. These four decide WHETHER your (even correct) message actually works.

**L25. Describe the 4 sentence types with two examples each.**
> **Ans:** **Declarative** (statement + .): "I study IT." / "Delhi is big." **Interrogative** (question + ?): "Where is the file?" / "Did you eat?" **Imperative** (order/request): "Open page 10." / "Please sit." **Exclamatory** (feeling + !): "What a goal!" / "How fast she runs!" Every sentence = Subject + Predicate with CAPITAL start.

**L26. Explain nouns, pronouns and verbs with examples, then write one sentence using all three.**
> **Ans:** **Noun** = name (Amit, Delhi, book). **Pronoun** = noun-replacer (he, she, they, it). **Verb** = action/state (run, is, write). Sentence: *"**Amit** (noun) **writes** (verb) fast, and **he** (pronoun) always finishes first."*

**L27. Explain adjectives, adverbs and prepositions with examples, then write one sentence using all three.**
> **Ans:** **Adjective** describes nouns (tall boy, red pen). **Adverb** describes verbs/adjectives (runs fast, very tall). **Preposition** shows relation (in, on, at, under). Sentence: *"The **tall** (adj) boy runs **quickly** (adv) **to** (prep) school."*

**L28. How do you write a good paragraph? Explain the 3 parts and write a 4-line paragraph on "Yoga".**
> **Ans:** **Parts:** ① Topic sentence (main idea) ② 3–4 supporting details/examples ③ Concluding line; one idea per paragraph. **Sample:** *"Yoga keeps students healthy. It stretches the body, calms the mind and sharpens focus. Just 20 minutes daily improves sleep and memory. So every student should practise yoga every morning."*

**L29. What are the two parts of a sentence? Identify subject and predicate in: (a) The little girl laughed loudly. (b) My father reads the newspaper. (c) Open your books.**
> **Ans:** **Subject** (who/what) + **Predicate** (what about them = verb + rest; the book also names verb + object inside it). (a) S: The little girl | P: laughed loudly (verb: laughed). (b) S: My father | P: reads the newspaper (verb: reads, object: newspaper). (c) S: (You — hidden) | P: Open your books.

**L30. Explain 6 punctuation marks with one use each.**
> **Ans:** **Full stop (.)** ends statements ("I came."). **Comma (,)** separates items/pauses ("apples, bananas"). **Question (?)** ends questions. **Exclamation (!)** shows feeling ("Wow!"). **Apostrophe (')** shows missing letters ("don't") and possession ("Ram's"). **Colon (:)** starts lists ("Bring: pen, file").

**L31. Compare oral and written communication on 4 points.**
> **Ans:** **Speed/feedback:** oral instant; written delayed. **Record:** oral vanishes; written is permanent proof. **Suitability:** oral for urgent/personal; written for formal/complex/far. **Skill needed:** oral needs speaking+listening; written needs reading+writing. Smart communicators mix both.

**L32. A shy student wants to participate more in class. Give 6 communication tips.**
> **Ans:** ① Prepare one question/answer daily (intrapersonal rehearsal). ② Sit in front; keep eye contact + nod (non-verbal confidence). ③ Start small — answer easy questions first. ④ Speak slowly and clearly (paralanguage). ⑤ Ask doubts without fear — teachers love feedback. ⑥ Join one group activity weekly (small-group practice).

## APPLICATION BASED — SET 2 (A13–A37)

**A13. Convert the vague feedback "Nice presentation" into specific + constructive feedback.**
> **Ans:** *"Nice presentation — your charts were clear (specific praise). Next time, slow down on slide 4 and add one example; the ending felt rushed (constructive fix)."* Names exact points + gives solutions.

**A14. Your friend never listens and always interrupts. Advise him in 3 points.**
> **Ans:** ① Let others finish — count to 3 before replying. ② Show listening: eye contact, nods, "So you mean…?" ③ Ask one question before giving your opinion. Listening doubles learning and friendships.

**A15. A notice says only "Meeting tomorrow." What is missing? Rewrite it fully.**
> **Ans:** Missing what/when-exact/where/whom (violates **Complete** + **Clear**). Rewrite: *"Eco-club meeting, Tue 16 Dec, 3 pm, Room 12. All members must attend with project files. — Secretary."*

**A16. The loudspeaker fails during assembly. How will you still inform 500 students quickly?**
> **Ans:** Switch channels fast: class captains pass the message orally row-to-row + write it BIG on the stage board (visual) + pin a written notice at the gate. Backup channels beat a dead speaker.

**A17. You must apologise to a teacher for late submission — oral or written? Why? Draft 2 lines.**
> **Ans:** **Both:** apologise orally first (shows respect + feelings), then submit with a short written note (record). Draft: *"Sorry Ma'am for the delay; I was unwell yesterday. I have completed the work and will submit early next time."*

**A18. A customer complains angrily on a call. Give 3 steps to handle it using communication skills.**
> **Ans:** ① Listen fully without interrupting (active listening melts anger). ② Paraphrase + apologise: "So the delivery broke — sorry, I understand." ③ Give a clear fix + deadline: "Replacement ships today, reaching Friday." Calm tone throughout.

**A19. Your class group has 200 unread messages with homework buried inside. Give 2 organising tips.**
> **Ans:** ① Ask admin to **pin** one daily homework message + ban forwards in study hours. ② One student posts a 5-line **summary** each night (concise + complete) — no more digging through 200 chats.

**A20. A blood-donation camp poster: which 5 details MUST it show?**
> **Ans:** The **Complete** set: ① WHAT (blood camp) ② WHEN (date + time) ③ WHERE (venue) ④ WHO (who can donate + contact) ⑤ WHY/benefit (certificate/refreshment). Missing any = failed poster.

**A21. Rewrite rudely-worded "Hey, send notes fast!" as a courteous formal request.**
> **Ans:** *"Hello Anu, could you please share yesterday's Science notes when free? I missed class due to fever. Thank you! — Ravi"* Adds greeting + please + reason + thanks = courteous + complete.

**A22. A classmate speaks too softly in presentations. Give 3 voice (paralanguage) tips.**
> **Ans:** ① Breathe deep; project to the LAST bench. ② Slow down + pause after key lines (speed control). ③ Stress important words with rising tone; record once and self-check. Loud + slow + stressed = heard.

**A23. You missed your bus stop wearing earphones. Which principle failed? Give the fix.**
> **Ans:** **Attention** failed — you blocked the conductor's announcements (channel). Fix: one earphone only + watch the route board; attention = receiving half of communication.

**A24. Two guests arrive: one Hindi-only, one English-only. How will you welcome both?**
> **Ans:** Beat the linguistic barrier three ways: greet EACH in their language ("Namaste! / Welcome!"), use universal gestures (smile, folded hands, pointing to seats/water), and seat a bilingual cousin between them as bridge.

**A25. Your exam handwriting is unreadable. Give 2 writing fixes.**
> **Ans:** ① Slow down 10% + leave a line gap between answers (legibility beats speed). ② Underline key terms + number points — examiner reads structure even if letters wobble. Practise one page daily.

**A26. The teacher's diagrams confuse you. Frame 2 feedback questions to ask for clarity.**
> **Ans:** ① *"Ma'am, could you please label parts A and B once more?"* (specific). ② *"So the arrow shows blood entering — am I right?"* (paraphrase-check). Specific + polite questions get the best re-explanations.

**A27. Debate: "Online classes are better." Which 2 methods will you use in the debate hall and why?**
> **Ans:** **Oral public speaking** (live arguments, rebuttals, voice power) + **visual** (one placard with 3 win-stats). Speaking persuades the judges; the visual fixes your strongest data in memory.

**A28. Draft a WhatsApp birthday invite for 50 relatives (keep it Complete).**
> **Ans:** *"Aarav turns 15! Dinner party Sun 21 Dec, 7 pm, Hotel Green Park, MG Road. Please confirm by 18 Dec — 98XXXXXX01. — Sharma family"* What+when+where+RSVP = complete, concise, courteous.

**A29. A stranger rudely demands your phone number. Refuse politely in 2 lines.**
> **Ans:** *"Sorry, I don't share my number with strangers. You can reach me through the school office."* Clear NO + polite tone + alternative = courteous refusal without rudeness.

**A30. Teach your grandmother to join a video call. Give steps using simple language + demo.**
> **Ans:** ① One step at a time in her language: "Press this GREEN button." ② Demo on your phone first, then guide her finger. ③ Paste a red sticker on the button (visual). ④ Practise twice; praise each success. Simple + slow + visual = learned.

**A31. Exam says "Attempt any 4 of 6". What does it mean? What if you attempt all 6?**
> **Ans:** Answer your BEST 4 only — choice lets you skip weak topics. Attempting all 6 wastes time; usually only the first 4 are checked, so extra answers earn nothing. Read instructions = part of communication skills.

**A32. Your essay is one 20-line paragraph. What is wrong? Fix it.**
> **Ans:** Wrong: many ideas crammed without breaks — unreadable wall of text. Fix: split into 4 paras (intro + 2 points + conclusion), each starting with a topic sentence. One idea per paragraph = coherent writing.

**A33. A friend forwards shocking "news" with no source. How will you respond + verify? (2 steps)**
> **Ans:** ① DON'T forward; reply *"Source? Let me verify first."* ② Check a trusted newspaper/site or official handle; share the verified link back. Correctness beats speed — forwarding lies spreads a harmful "message".

**A34. A boss says "Be creative!" but rejects every new idea. Which principle is violated? Advise him.**
> **Ans:** **Consistency** violated — words ("be creative") clash with actions (reject all). Advice: either welcome trial ideas with clear limits ("pitch within Rs. 5,000") or honestly say "follow the fixed process". Words must match deeds.

**A35. As class monitor, draft a 3-line polite fees reminder announcement.**
> **Ans:** *"Good morning all. Please submit January fees (Rs. 2,500) to the office by Friday 3 pm and collect receipts. Thank you for cooperating! — Monitor, XA"* Clear + complete + courteous in 3 lines.

**A36. Your cousin copies your homework daily. Say NO politely but firmly in 2 lines.**
> **Ans:** *"I can't share daily homework — you'll never learn it yourself. Sit with me for 20 minutes; I'll explain Q1–Q3."* Firm NO + helpful alternative = courteous boundary.

**A37. In a noisy playground, a coach shouts scores vs uses whistle + gesture board. Which works and why?**
> **Ans:** **Whistle + gesture board wins.** Shouting fights the physical barrier (noise) and fails; sharp whistle (sound signal) + BIG visual board cross noise and distance — right channel beats loud effort.

## COMPETENCY BASED — SET 2 (C13–C37)

**C13. Priya reads her full speech from paper, keeps eyes down, and speaks in a flat monotone. The audience starts chatting. (a) Identify 3 mistakes. (b) Give 3 fixes.**
> **Ans: (a)** Full-script reading (no eye contact) • zero expressions (dead non-verbal) • monotone (poor paralanguage). **(b)** Bullet-point cards + look up often • smile, gestures, move slightly • vary tone/speed, pause after punch lines.

**C14. A shopkeeper and a foreign tourist share no language. Using gestures, a calculator for prices, samples to touch, and a big smile, the sale succeeds. (a) Name the methods used. (b) State the lesson.**
> **Ans: (a)** **Non-verbal** (gestures, smile) + **visual** (calculator digits, samples). **(b)** Lesson: when the linguistic barrier blocks words, non-verbal + visual bridges it — patience and creativity complete the sale.

**C15. Notice A: "Fee. Last date soon." Notice B: "January fees Rs. 2,500 — pay at office counter 2 by Fri 3 pm; late fine Rs. 50/day. — Office" (a) Which is better? (b) Which Cs are missing in A?**
> **Ans: (a)** **Notice B** — exact and actionable. **(b)** A lacks **Clear** (how much? where?), **Complete** (no date/venue), **Concrete** (no figures) and **Courteous** tone. Vague notices create queues of confused parents.

**C16. Rahul forwards "Exam postponed!" (a rumour) on WhatsApp; panic spreads. The teacher then issues a signed circular: "Exam on schedule." (a) What went wrong with channel + correctness? (b) What was the right fix?**
> **Ans: (a)** Rumour via informal chat = wrong channel + unverified (incorrect) message; speed without truth. **(b)** Official **written circular** from authority — correct + complete + trusted channel — killed the rumour. Lesson: verify before forwarding.

**C17. In online class, a student's mic stays ON (house noise), camera stays OFF, and he types "ok" for every answer. Suggest 3 netiquette fixes.**
> **Ans:** ① Mic OFF except when speaking (kills physical barrier/noise). ② Camera ON + sit straight (non-verbal presence + attention). ③ Type/speak FULL answers ("Q3 = 42 because…") — "ok" is vague, incomplete feedback.

**C18. A cricket captain uses short clear calls ("Mine!", "Two!") plus hand signals for field changes. The team never collides. Why is this effective? (2 reasons)**
> **Ans:** (1) **Clear + concise oral** codes decide in one second — no confusion mid-run. (2) **Visual hand signals** cross crowd noise and distance (physical barrier). Pre-decided codes = zero decoding time.

**C19. A hospital receptionist speaks softly, points to signboards, and gives numbered tokens. (a) Name 3 methods used. (b) Why does this combo suit a hospital?**
> **Ans: (a)** **Oral** (soft speech) + **visual** (signboards, tokens) + **non-verbal** (pointing, calm tone). **(b)** Patients are stressed/sick (emotional barrier) — soft + visual + tokens need no shouting, no language mastery, and keep order silent.

**C20. At a crowded fair, a lost child's announcement gives: name, age, dress colour, and "collect at Gate 2 counter". Why does this combo work? Name the methods.**
> **Ans:** **Oral** loudspeaker (instant mass reach) + **complete details** (concrete description finds the child fast) + fixed **written/displayed** meeting point (no confusion). Urgent + emotional situation = fast oral + exact details + one clear action.

**C21. A notice reads: "all students assemble ground tomorrow bring you id cards" (a) List 5 writing mistakes. (b) Rewrite it correctly. (c) Name the sentence type of your rewrite.**
> **Ans: (a)** Small-letter start (needs capital A) • no full stops • missing "on the" before ground • "you" → "your" (wrong word) • two ideas jammed in one breath. **(b)** "All students assemble on the ground tomorrow. Bring your ID cards." **(c)** **Imperative** (orders).

**C22. An old grandfather cannot read tiny medicine labels and mixes up pills. Suggest 3 communication fixes for the family.**
> **Ans:** ① Rewrite schedule BIG on one chart (morning/afternoon/night). ② Colour stickers: red = morning, blue = night (visual coding). ③ Demo once + daily oral reminder call; keep pills in labelled boxes. Big + colour + spoken = safe.

**C23. A new student stays silent for a week — new city, shy, afraid of wrong English. Suggest 3 teacher steps to include him.**
> **Ans:** ① Warm welcome + buddy partner for a week (emotional safety). ② Invite easy answers first + praise every attempt (builds confidence). ③ Allow Hindi initially; correct gently in private (linguistic bridge, courteous feedback).

**C24. In a traffic jam, Driver A honks continuously; Driver B uses indicators, keeps distance and waits. Whose communication is better and why?**
> **Ans:** **Driver B.** Honking = noise (physical barrier) + rudeness communicating only anger; indicators + patience = clear visual signals + courteous behaviour that others understand and copy. Good communication calms; bad spreads stress.

**C25. A science model with live demo + charts + Q&A beats a poster-only model at the exhibition. Why? (2 reasons)**
> **Ans:** (1) **Multi-method** (oral explanation + visual demo + interaction) suits every learner type. (2) **Live Q&A feedback** proves real understanding, while a static poster is one-way and forgettable. Show + tell + discuss wins.

**C26. A library needs pin-drop silence; a playground is full of shouting. How does the environment change the method choice in each place?**
> **Ans:** **Library:** silence rule → written notes, whispers, gestures (oral banned). **Playground:** noise (physical barrier) → whistle, BIG gestures, boards (soft speech fails). Same message, different channel — environment dictates the method.

**C27. Two siblings fight daily over the TV remote. Apply 3 communication steps to solve it peacefully.**
> **Ans:** ① **Listen** to each side fully without interrupting (active listening). ② **Paraphrase** needs: "So you want cartoons 6–7, you want match 7–8?" ③ **Agree + write** a timetable on paper (written record) + weekly swap. Talk → confirm → record = peace.

**C28. Hotel review A: "Bad!" Review B: "Room clean but AC noisy in Room 204; please service it." Which feedback is useful and why?**
> **Ans:** **Review B.** A is vague + only negative (no action possible). B is specific (Room 204, AC) + constructive (service it) + balanced (praises cleanliness) — the hotel can ACT on it. Specificity = usefulness.

**C29. A candidate gives election speeches AND distributes pamphlets. Give one strength of EACH and explain why combining wins.**
> **Ans:** **Speech (oral/public):** emotion + persuasion + live crowd energy. **Pamphlet (written+visual):** promises stay as proof voters re-read. Combined = heart (speech) + mind/record (pamphlet) — either alone is weaker.

**C30. During a fire drill: alarm rings, teachers use hand gestures for lines, and a board shows the assembly point map. Name the 3 methods and explain why ALL three are needed.**
> **Ans:** **Alarm** (sound signal) + **gestures** (non-verbal) + **map board** (visual). Panic + noise kill speech (barriers); alarm alerts instantly, gestures guide without words, map shows exactly WHERE — emergency = triple channel, zero confusion.

**C31. A class includes a deaf student. The teacher writes key points on the board, uses gestures, and a buddy shares notes. Name the methods + praise 2 good practices.**
> **Ans:** **Written** (board/notes) + **visual/non-verbal** (gestures). Good practices: ① multiple channels so disability never blocks learning (inclusive design); ② buddy system = peer feedback + emotional belonging. Barrier removed by method-switching.

**C32. Q3 in the exam paper has a printing error. The invigilator announces the correction orally AND writes it on the board. Why are both needed?**
> **Ans:** **Oral** alerts all 40 students instantly (speed); **board writing** stays visible for re-checking till the end (record). Oral-only would be forgotten/misheard; written-only might be missed by late readers — critical corrections need both.

**C33. A family shouts over picnic-spot choices until father lists 4 options on paper and takes votes. Which technique fixed the chaos?**
> **Ans:** **Organised written + feedback technique:** listing (written/visual) made options clear and finite; voting collected everyone's feedback fairly. Chaos (overlapping oral) → order (seen + counted). Writing + voting tames shouting.

**C34. For learning origami, a YouTube tutorial beats the book chapter. Why? (2 reasons) Also give ONE advantage books still hold.**
> **Ans:** Video wins: (1) **visual + oral together** — see folds + hear tips simultaneously; (2) **pause/replay** = self-paced feedback loop. Book's edge: permanent, searchable, no internet/battery needed — best as quick reference AFTER learning.

**C35. The boss orders: "Dispatch these today." The peon delivers tomorrow, saying "today" meant "sometime soon". Which C failed? Rewrite the order.**
> **Ans:** **Clear + Complete** failed — "today" is vague (by when? to whom? how?). Rewrite: *"Dispatch these 5 parcels by DTDC before 5 pm today to Agra; share the receipt photo."* Exact what/when/how = no excuses.

**C36. Morning assembly has prayer, a spoken thought, news reading and instructions. Name the methods used and explain why assembly suits all of them.**
> **Ans:** **Oral/public** (prayer, thought, news, instructions) + **non-verbal** (attention posture, silence) + sometimes **visual** (charts/awards). Assembly = whole school, silent, attentive (no barriers) — one event delivering values + info + orders to all at once.

**C37. A star meets a huge fan crowd and only smiles, waves, folds hands and says "Thank you!" — no speech. Yet fans leave happy. Why is this enough?**
> **Ans:** **Non-verbal power + courtesy:** smile/wave/namaste speak warmth louder than words; "thank you" adds courtesy. A speech was impossible (noise, time, languages) — so the star chose universal wordless signals + one grateful line. Right method for the moment.
