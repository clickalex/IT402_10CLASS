Unit 1 — Communication Skills-II
SHORT — 2 marks (20–30 words)

S1. Differentiate oral and written communication with one advantage each.

Ans: Oral = spoken words (phone, speech); advantage: fast with instant feedback. Written = letters/emails; advantage: permanent record that can be re-read.

S2. Why is feedback significant? Give two points.

Ans: (1) It confirms the message was understood correctly. (2) It corrects errors early and improves future performance of sender and receiver.

S3. Explain any two Cs of effective communication with examples.

Ans: (1) Clear — simple, exact message ("Submit the file by Friday 5 pm"). (2) Courteous — polite words ("please", "thank you", "sorry").

S4. State two rules of good paragraph writing.

Ans: (1) Start with a topic sentence stating the main idea. (2) Add 3–4 supporting details plus one concluding line; keep one idea per paragraph.
LONG — 4 marks (50–80 words)

L1. Describe the three methods of communication with examples. How do you choose the right one?

Ans: Verbal: oral (phone, speech — fast, no record) + written (email, report — permanent proof). Non-verbal: expressions, gestures, tone — simplest, body beats words. Visual: charts, signals, posters — crosses languages, best for ads. Choice depends on urgency, distance, audience and record need: urgent+nearby → oral; formal proof → written; feelings → face-to-face; language differs → visual.

L2. Explain any four barriers to communication with one example and one measure each.

Ans: (1) Physical — class noise → quiet room, working mic. (2) Linguistic — French manual → simple words + visuals. (3) Cultural — 👍 rude abroad → learn customs. (4) Emotional — anger blocks listening → stay calm, choose right time. General rules: active listening, right medium, feedback, summarise key points.
APPLICATION — use the concept

A1. Riya messages her team "do it soon". Identify the violated C and rewrite the message correctly.

Ans: Violates Clear (vague, no deadline) and Complete (no what/when). Rewrite: "Please submit the sales sheet (what) by Friday 5 pm (when) on email (where)." — now clear, complete and courteous.

A2. A team leader's juniors look confused by his technical jargon. What two steps should he take?

Ans: (1) Replace jargon with simple words + show a chart/demo (attack the linguistic barrier). (2) Pause, ask "any doubts?", and paraphrase replies to confirm (feedback + active listening).
COMPETENCY / CASE-STUDY

C1. Read and answer:

Amit presents with zero eye contact, 40 text-packed slides in 10 minutes, and says "no questions, I am getting late". The manager understands nothing. (a) Identify 4 communication mistakes. (b) Suggest 4 fixes.
Ans: (a) No eye contact (poor non-verbal) • overloaded slides (not concise) • too fast (no clarity) • no questions (blocks feedback). (b) Eye contact + open posture • max 10 slides, 1 idea each • slow, clear speech • invite questions + summarise at end.

C2. Read and answer:

For the school fest, Class X puts up posters at shops, announces on loudspeaker, and the principal sends a written notice to parents. (a) Name the 3 methods used. (b) Why is combining them better than one?
Ans: (a) Posters = visual; loudspeaker = oral/verbal; notice = written. (b) Visual attracts + oral spreads fast + written is proof — together they cover illiterate/nuanced/busy audiences with speed AND record.
